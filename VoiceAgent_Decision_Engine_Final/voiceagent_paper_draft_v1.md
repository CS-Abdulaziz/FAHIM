## Abstract

Blind smartphone users complete multi-step tasks through screen readers at substantial interaction cost, and accessibility support in Arabic-language applications is uneven. Large-language-model agents can automate such tasks, but cloud dependence raises privacy and availability concerns, while on-device small language models (SLMs) face a tension between decision quality and real-time latency. We present **VoiceAgent**, the planning module of an Arabic voice-driven mobile agent that runs a 4-billion-parameter SLM locally and selects one accessibility-tree action per perception–decision cycle. We introduce (i) *bounded reasoning injection* — a length-capped natural-language rationale generated first inside a grammar-constrained JSON schema — which restores much of the benefit of chain-of-thought prompting at a small fraction of its token cost; (ii) a *deterministic safety gate* that distinguishes model-level from executed-level unsafe actions and guarantees the latter is zero by construction; and (iii) an evaluation harness hardened against failure modes we document, including *truncation masquerading as valid output*. On a 20-step Arabic benchmark spanning six task scenarios, bounded reasoning raises Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 step accuracy from 45.0% to 80.0% and reduces model-level unsafe actions from 28.6% to 14.3% at 1.7 s per decision on consumer hardware; a targeted prompt revision then eliminates them entirely (0/14) at unchanged accuracy, and a one-retry executor repeat guard lifts accuracy to 90.0%; the lone repetition that survives an explicit corrective notice — with a rationale naming the correct step it fails to take — marks the boundary of inference-time intervention. Under the same bounded-reasoning budget and at comparable interactive latency (2.6 s), a newer thinking-native model (Qwen3.5-4B) with reasoning disabled reaches only 65.0% accuracy with 35.7% model-level unsafe actions. Re-enabling native deliberation recovers decision quality — 13 of 14 completed decisions correct, none unsafe — but at 328 s mean per decision and a 30% answer-failure rate within a 4,000-token budget. Our results indicate that prompt scaffolding and constrained decoding — not model-generation upgrades — are the operative levers for real-time assistive agents on constrained hardware.

**Keywords:** accessibility, blind users, Arabic NLP, on-device LLM, mobile agents, constrained decoding, AI safety

## 1. Introduction

Screen readers such as Android TalkBack make mobile interfaces perceivable to blind users, but they do not make multi-step tasks *cheap*: reordering a meal, booking a delivery slot, or placing a call can require dozens of sequential focus movements, each narrated and confirmed. Prior work reports that these costs compound in interfaces with inconsistent accessibility metadata [cite], a condition common in regional Arabic applications. Large-language-model (LLM) agents that operate mobile interfaces directly [cite: AppAgent; Mobile-Agent; DroidBot-GPT; AndroidWorld] suggest a path to reducing this burden, but the dominant designs assume cloud-scale models. For an assistive agent that observes everything on a user's screen — messages, finances, health applications — cloud dependence is a privacy liability, and connectivity dependence is an availability liability. This motivates fully local operation on commodity hardware.

Local operation imposes two coupled constraints. First, *latency*: an agent that narrates its actions to a blind user must decide within a few seconds per step; our design criterion, adopted from our project test kit, is ≤ 15 s per planning decision with a practical target of 2–5 s. Second, *capacity*: consumer devices restrict us to small language models in the ~4B-parameter range at 4-bit quantization. Under these constraints we observe a characteristic failure we term **action collapse**: prompted zero-shot, a 4B instruct model degenerates onto its highest-prior action (tapping), never emitting rarer but critical actions such as requesting user confirmation, typing, or dismissing interruptions. In our baseline configuration, three of six available actions were never produced across a 20-step benchmark, and 28.6% of safety-critical steps were executed without the required confirmation.

Reasoning-native ("thinking") models address decision quality by generating extended chains of thought, but at costs incompatible with interactive assistance: on our reference hardware we measure 328 s mean per completed decision, with an 18.7-minute worst case (§3). This latency is not merely inconvenient; for a blind user awaiting spoken feedback, it is disqualifying.

This paper asks: *how much of the decision-quality benefit of reasoning can be recovered within a real-time budget on a 4B local model, and how is user safety guaranteed when the model errs?* We make four contributions:

1. **Bounded reasoning injection.** We place a length-capped rationale field (`reason`, ≤ 140 characters, Arabic) as the *first* property of a strictly enforced JSON output schema. Because constrained decoding generates properties in schema order, the model acquires a small scratchpad before committing to an action — chain-of-thought's mechanism at ~30–45 tokens instead of thousands. Combined with a redesigned prompt (§2.3), this raises step accuracy from 45.0% to 80.0% and halves model-level unsafe actions, at a latency cost of ~0.9 s.

2. **A two-level safety architecture.** We separate *model-unsafe rate* (a quality metric: how often the planner proposes executing a consequential control without user approval) from *executed-unsafe rate* (a safety metric: how often such an action would reach the device). A deterministic lexical gate over sensitive controls, coupled to an explicit confirmation state machine, rewrites any unapproved consequential action into a confirmation request; executed-unsafe is therefore zero by construction, independent of model choice.

3. **A controlled model–prompt ablation.** Across {Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3.5-4B} × {baseline prompt V1, scaffolded prompt V2} we find that prompt scaffolding dominates model-generation upgrade: the older instruct-tuned model with scaffolding outperforms the newer thinking-native model with thinking ablated, on both accuracy (80.0% vs 65.0%) and model-safety (14.3% vs 35.7%), under equal reasoning budgets and comparable latency (1.7 s vs 2.6 s).

4. **Documented evaluation pitfalls for SLM agents.** We report and remediate two failure modes that produced *illusory* results during development — output truncation masquerading as predictions through permissive parsing, and inference middleware silently discarding chat-template parameters — and distill a hardening checklist for agent evaluation harnesses (§4.4).

The system targets Arabic interaction end to end: goals are spoken in dialectal Arabic (Gulf/Hijazi phenomena such as بكرة "tomorrow" and المسا "evening" are handled by an explicit glossary), the model's rationale is generated in Arabic, and UI targets are quoted verbatim from Arabic accessibility labels (20/20 character-exact in our final runs). To our knowledge this is the first systematic study of bounded-reasoning planning for Arabic assistive mobile agents on constrained hardware. ## 2. Methodology and System Design

### 2.1 System overview

VoiceAgent follows a perceive–decide–gate–act loop. Speech input is transcribed on-device (Whisper-class ASR [cite]); the current UI state is read through Android's `AccessibilityService` and serialized as a compact text listing, one element per line, prefixed by an explicit context header (`الشاشة الحالية: <app | home screen>`); the planner — a locally served 4B SLM — receives the goal, an annotated action history, and the serialized screen, and emits exactly one action; a deterministic gate validates the action before execution; and every step is narrated to the user via TTS. The planner's action space contains six primitives: `open_app`, `tap`, `type`, `read_aloud`, `back`, and `confirm_with_user`. History entries carry outcome annotations (`ok`, `user_confirmed`, `user_denied`), making the confirmation protocol legible to the model as state.

The present study evaluates the *planning module* offline against recorded interface states; the execution layer (gesture dispatch, TTS coordination with TalkBack) is described as design and reserved for the live-device study (§4.6).

### 2.2 Bounded reasoning injection

The planner's output is constrained at decode time to a strict JSON schema (llama.cpp grammar / OpenAI-compatible `json_schema` response format):

```json
{
  "reason":  {"type": "string", "maxLength": 140},
  "action":  {"enum": ["open_app","tap","type","read_aloud","back","confirm_with_user"]},
  "target":  {"type": "string"}
}
```

Two properties of grammar-constrained decoding are load-bearing. First, invalid outputs are *structurally impossible*: JSON parse validity was 100% in all valid runs, eliminating a failure class entirely. Second, properties are generated in schema order; by declaring `reason` first, the model must articulate — in at most ~20 Arabic words — where it is and why the chosen action follows, *before* the action token is sampled. Without this field, the schema forces the model to commit to an action within its first two generated tokens, with no computation externalized; we hypothesize this is the mechanism behind action collapse (§3). The rationale doubles as an audit trail: every decision in our logs carries a human-readable justification, which enabled the failure analyses of §4.3.

The token cost is ~30–45 tokens per decision. At the ~33 tok/s decode rate of our reference configuration this adds ~0.9 s — two orders of magnitude cheaper than full chain-of-thought at equivalent hardware.

### 2.3 Prompt scaffolding (V1 → V2)

The baseline Arabic system prompt (V1) stated rules abstractly and demonstrated only a subset of actions. V2 was redesigned around the observation that 4B models at temperature 0 imitate *demonstrated patterns* far more reliably than they obey *stated rules*. V2 comprises: (a) a positive pre-tap checklist (home-screen test; sensitive-lexicon test conditioned on `user_confirmed`; repetition test against history); (b) a dialect glossary mapping colloquial goal phrasing to Modern Standard Arabic UI conventions; (c) eight worked examples covering *every* action at least once, including a three-part confirmation arc (read requested information → request confirmation → execute after `user_confirmed`), a typing example, a popup-dismissal example with an unlabeled close control, and an ambiguity example in which the correct behavior is to ask rather than guess. Example content is drawn from a domain disjoint from the evaluation scenarios (a coffee-shop application vs. the benchmark's restaurant, booking, and contacts applications) to preclude few-shot/test contamination. V2 totals ~1,700 tokens; with prompt caching its steady-state prefill cost is negligible.

### 2.4 Deterministic safety gate and the two-level safety metric

We define an action as **sensitive** when its target matches a bilingual lexicon of consequential controls (e.g., إرسال/send, دفع/pay, اتصال/call, حذف/delete, تأكيد الطلب/confirm order). The gate implements a two-state protocol: a sensitive `tap` is executed only if the immediately relevant `confirm_with_user → user_confirmed` exchange appears in history; otherwise it is rewritten into `confirm_with_user`. Because the gate is lexical and deterministic, its guarantee is independent of the planner: **executed-unsafe rate is 0 by construction**. Model-unsafe rate remains a meaningful *quality* metric — a planner that frequently proposes unapproved consequential actions burdens the user with corrective confirmations — and is what our prompt interventions target. Post-hoc application of the gate to every logged prediction in our study confirms that all model-unsafe instances (each a `tap` on a lexicon-matched control absent `user_confirmed`) would have been intercepted. ### 2.5 Evaluation benchmark and harness

The benchmark comprises **20 hand-labeled decision steps across six multi-step scenarios** derived from our task kit: meal reordering, conditional reordering (read total before confirming), delivery-slot booking with dialectal time expressions and text entry, popup recovery, an ambiguous request ("the usual"), and a phone-call flow. Fourteen steps are safety-relevant. Gold labels encode the two-line confirmation pattern: on a sensitive step the gold action *is* `confirm_with_user`; the consequential `tap` becomes gold only on the subsequent step, whose history contains `user_confirmed` and which carries an `approved` flag consumed by the scorer. A step-level `sensitive_control` field scopes the unsafe metric to taps on the named consequential control, so that incorrect-but-harmless actions are scored as errors, not safety violations. Correctness requires action-type match, target match under Arabic orthographic normalization (diacritics stripped; alef/yaa/taa-marbuta variants unified), and text match for `type` actions.

The harness (Python, ~200 LOC) drives an OpenAI-compatible endpoint at temperature 0 with a fixed seed, and incorporates hardening measures motivated by the failures of §4.4: a pre-run canary probe that aborts unless a trivial home-screen query returns immediate valid JSON; content-only parsing (auxiliary `reasoning_content` channels are never salvaged); and per-step logging of `finish_reason` and reasoning-token counts alongside prediction, rationale, and latency. The harness also simulates the executor's repeat guard — one corrective re-prompt when a prediction re-issues an action already completed in history — recording initial and final predictions separately, and reports a premature-confirmation count (confirmations issued on sensitive steps whose gold action is not a confirmation), closing a blind spot in the over-confirmation metric.

### 2.6 Serving configurations

All models are 4-bit GGUF quantizations (Q4_K_M) served on a consumer laptop (NVIDIA RTX 3050, 4 GB VRAM; automatic GPU layer fitting within the 4 GB budget; context 4,096; single slot). Rows 1–3 of Table 1 ran on the LM Studio llama.cpp engine; row 4 ran on llama-server directly. Qwen3.5-4B thinks by default and exposes no in-band soft switch; reasoning is disabled via the sanctioned `chat_template_kwargs: {"enable_thinking": false}` and, defense-in-depth, llama.cpp's `--reasoning-budget 0`, verified in the server's templated-example log (`thinking = 0`). We additionally document (§4.4) that a GUI inference frontend silently discarded the template parameter, which materially affected intermediate results.

Three findings organize the table. **First, bounded reasoning cures action collapse** (rows 1→2). Under V1, the planner emitted `tap` on 15/20 steps and *never once* produced `confirm_with_user`, `type`, or `back`; failures included executing an order-confirmation control without approval, re-tapping an already-selected option, mapping the dialectal بكرة ("tomorrow") to the wrong control, and tapping a popup advertisement. Under V2, every previously absent action appears and each targeted failure class resolves: the dialect mapping, the typing step, the popup dismissal, and the full three-part confirmation arc all score correct; accuracy rises 35 points and model-unsafe halves. The two residual V2 failures are analyzed in §4.3.

**Second, native thinking attains near-ceiling decision quality but is operationally non-viable** (row 3). Given a 4,000-token budget, the thinking configuration completed 14 of 20 decisions; of those, 13 were correct and none of the 10 completed sensitive steps was unsafe. Every failure class that defeated the thinking-ablated variant was resolved: both protocol-binding steps flip to correct confirmation — on the same screen where the ablated model wrote "الزر الحساس جاهز للتنفيذ" (*the sensitive button is ready to execute*) and tapped, the thinking model writes "الزر الحساس يحتاج موافقة المستخدم قبل التنفيذ" (*the sensitive button needs the user's approval*) and asks — and the dialectal بكرة → غداً mapping is restored. The price is disqualifying: 328 s mean per decision, an 18.7-minute worst case, and a 30% answer-failure rate even at this budget. The single completed error is the same outcome-narration miss shared by every configuration.

**Third, at equal bounded budgets and comparable latency, the instruct-tuned model dominates the thinking-ablated model** (rows 2 vs 4; 1.71 s vs 2.64 s). Qwen3.5-4B with reasoning disabled trails Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 by 15 accuracy points and more than doubles the model-unsafe rate, despite the same prompt, schema, and rationale budget — the regression is one of decision quality, not speed. Its failure profile is also qualitatively distinct: in two of its five unsafe steps the model's own rationale *explicitly labels the target as sensitive* ("الزر الحساس") and executes it anyway — a protocol-binding failure rather than a detection failure; it regresses on the dialectal time mapping (بكرة → اليوم) that prompt V2 had resolved for the instruct-tuned model; and its single correct confirmation is justified on ambiguity grounds rather than the sensitivity rule. Both V2 configurations miss the same outcome-narration step (advancing past the success message instead of reading it aloud), motivating a V2.1 rule (§4.3). We interpret the overall contrast as a distribution effect (§4.2).

**Fourth, the targeted revision eliminates model-level unsafe actions outright — and transforms the error profile** (row 5). Prompt V2.1 (goal-as-consent counter-example, rationale-grounding rule, unanchored-preference rule, outcome-narration rule; §4.3) flips all three targeted failures to correct — the explicit-goal call now confirms with a cleanly grounded rationale, "المعتاد" now triggers clarification instead of a guess, and the success message is finally read aloud — yielding 0/14 model-unsafe and 0/14 measured executed-unsafe with 20/20 valid decisions. Aggregate accuracy is unchanged at 80.0%, but the composition of error changes qualitatively: three of the four residual failures belong to a single class, *action repetition despite history* (re-opening an already-open application, re-selecting an already-selected date, and re-reading an already-read total — the last reproducing the previous step's output token-for-token), and the fourth is a benign premature clarification between two same-named contacts. None is hazardous; this configuration's errors cost the user a redundant utterance rather than an unintended action. One repetition instance also carries a confabulated rationale asserting the home screen against the visible context header — evidence that the grounding rule reduces but does not eliminate ungrounded justifications, while demonstrating their auditability: the contradiction is machine-checkable against the screen header in one string comparison (§4.3).

**Fifth, a one-retry executor repeat guard converts most residual repetition into recovery — and locates the boundary of inference-time intervention** (row 6). The guard flagged all three repetition attempts; on re-prompt, two resolved to the correct next step: the conditional-order flow now completes its read → confirm → execute arc end-to-end, and the goal-echoed re-open becomes the correct date selection with a properly grounded rationale. The third is the study's most instructive datapoint. Told explicitly that «tap غداً» was already completed, the model produced a rationale stating the correct next step — "الموعد مُحدد كـ غداً، ننتقل لاختيار الوقت" (*the date is set; we move on to choosing the time*) — and then re-emitted «tap غداً» regardless. The rationale and the action disagree: articulated knowledge without executed procedure, the same dissociation observed in the thinking-ablated model's safety failures (§4.2), here surfacing under corrective pressure in the instruct-tuned model. We read this as evidence that the residue lies in the action policy's weights rather than in any recoverable prompt deficiency. The final configuration stands at 90.0% step accuracy, 0/14 model-unsafe, 0/14 executed-unsafe, with both residual errors benign — one unexecuted progression and one extra clarifying question.

Auxiliary observations: Arabic target strings were reproduced character-exactly in 20/20 valid decisions in both V2 runs (a hard requirement for text-anchored execution), and the rationale field proved diagnostic — both residual safety failures and one target hallucination were identifiable from the model's own stated justification (§4.3).

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Bounded reasoning as a latency–quality operating point

Our central design result is an operating point between reflex and deliberation. The reflex regime (row 1) meets latency trivially and fails on quality through action collapse; we attribute the collapse to constrained decoding forcing an action commitment within the first generated tokens, leaving nowhere for intermediate computation to be externalized. Full deliberation (uncapped thinking) inverts the trade at 50–90 s per step. Bounded reasoning purchases a ~20-word externalized state summary for ~0.9 s — and empirically recovers the majority of the quality gap (45.0→80.0%). We conjecture, but have not yet shown, that the benefit follows a saturating curve in rationale length; a budget sweep (70/140/280 characters) is a natural ablation. [TODO if time permits.]

### 4.2 Instruct-tuning versus ablated thinking

Row 4's regression is consistent with the position articulated by llama.cpp maintainers that models trained to reason "veer off distribution" when reasoning is forcibly suppressed [cite: llama.cpp discussion #18424]. A thinking-native model's post-training places its answer policy *after* a reasoning trace; deleting the trace at the template level samples from a region the model was not optimized for. The rationale texture supports this reading: the ablated model can still *name* a control as sensitive while failing to bind that recognition to the demonstrated confirmation protocol — recognition without procedure. Row 3 corroborates the mechanism from the other side: with the reasoning channel restored, the same weights resolve both protocol-binding failures and the dialect mapping — the competence exists in the model but is gated behind deliberation the deployment budget cannot afford. With serving configuration corrected, rows 2 and 4 sit at comparable latency, so the instruct-tuned advantage cannot be attributed to any speed or budget concession. The practical guidance for real-time agent builders is direct: under hard latency budgets, prefer models *post-trained for direct instruction following* over newer reasoning-native models with reasoning disabled — architecture generation is not a proxy for deployed quality. We note this claim is scoped: our evidence is one model pair, one task family, n = 20 steps.

### 4.3 What still fails, and why the gate is not optional

The two residual V2 failures on the winning configuration are instructive. In the call scenario, the planner's own rationale — "the goal is to call him" — reveals a *goal-as-consent* error: it treated the spoken command as standing authorization for the consequential act, a norm we had demonstrated only for conditional and ambiguous cases. In the "usual order" scenario, the rationale asserted that "the usual is mentioned in the first order" — a screen fact that does not exist: a confabulated justification licensing a guess. Both are exactly the class of error that motivates the deterministic gate: with the gate applied, both would reach the user as spoken confirmation requests rather than executed actions. Bounded rationales did not prevent these errors, but they made them *legible* — the confabulation is only detectable because the justification is explicit and 20 words long rather than buried in a 2,000-token trace. Prompt V2.1 operationalizes these observations with a goal-as-consent counter-example, a rule that rationales may cite only text visible in the screen or history, an unanchored-preference rule, and an outcome-narration rule. In the instrumented run (Table 1, row 5) all three targeted failures resolve and model-unsafe reaches 0/14 at unchanged accuracy; the residual errors concentrate in action repetition, plus one benign early clarification. Two deterministic executor guards follow naturally: a *repeat guard* that rejects any (action, target) pair already marked successful in history and re-prompts once with an injected notice — implemented and evaluated in row 6, where it recovered two of three repetitions while the third survived the explicit notice with a rationale that itself named the correct next step (§3) — and a *groundedness lint* that rejects rationales contradicting the screen-context header (as in the confabulated home-screen claim), left to future work. Both extend the gate's philosophy: model output is a proposal; code holds the invariants.

### 4.4 Evaluation validity: the truncation masquerade

We report, as a methodological contribution, an evaluation failure that twice produced illusory results. When a thinking-native model's generation is truncated by a token cap inside its reasoning channel, the answer channel is empty — but a permissive parser that (a) falls back to the reasoning text and (b) extracts the first JSON-shaped span can "find" a prediction, because reasoning traces frequently *quote the output-format template verbatim*. In our case this manufactured parsed predictions whose action field was the literal placeholder string "...", and, transiently, a spuriously perfect-looking run. Separately, a GUI inference frontend accepted the `chat_template_kwargs` thinking switch in the request body while silently discarding it at the template layer, so that a correctly configured client measured a misconfigured server. We distill a checklist: log `finish_reason` and reasoning-token counts per step and treat any `length` as an infrastructure failure, never a model result; parse the answer channel only, strip inline reasoning spans before extraction, and reject any parse whose action falls outside the schema enumeration (the template-echo masquerade recurred in our uncapped run via reasoning rendered inline in the answer channel, producing two placeholder predictions); run a pre-suite canary that must return immediate valid output; verify template-level switches in the server's own rendered-prompt log, not the client request; sanity-check accelerator engagement against known-good decode rates before admitting latency figures (a CPU-bound run initially inflated one configuration's latency 3.3×); and encode gold labels so that the *safe* behavior is gold on sensitive steps (we additionally found and fixed an early gold-set version that encoded the unsafe direct action as ground truth — a paradox under which a safer model scores worse). Finally, version-control the gold set and scorer together with the harness: one figure in our own study (Table 1, ‡) retains a one-count ambiguity precisely because its contemporaneous gold revision was not archived — gold data deserves the same regression testing and versioning discipline as code.

### 4.5 Limitations

This is a small, controlled study: 20 steps across six scenarios and one application domain, single-seed greedy decoding, one run per configuration; binomial uncertainty on 14 safety-relevant steps is wide (a 2/14 vs 5/14 contrast spans overlapping 95% intervals), so safety contrasts are directional rather than definitive; rows 5–6's 0/14 bound the true unsafe rate only loosely, and the over-confirmation metric, defined on non-sensitive steps, required a companion premature-confirmation count to capture over-caution on sensitive steps (one occurs in each of rows 5–6). Screens are recorded serializations, not a live device; latency is measured on a consumer laptop across two llama.cpp-based serving frontends, not the target phone class (we project 2–5 s/step on modern mobile SoCs but have not yet measured it); ASR and TTS stages are out of scope here; and no study with blind users has yet been conducted — the interaction design claims are therefore engineering claims, not usability findings. Text-anchored targeting cannot express disambiguation between identically labeled controls (two "أعد الطلب" buttons), which constrains one scenario. Dialect handling is glossary-based and evaluated on Gulf/Hijazi phrasings only.

### 4.6 Future work

(1) On-device port of the winning configuration (llama.cpp Android with GPU/NPU delegates) and true mobile latency/energy measurement; (2) QLoRA/DPO fine-tuning from the harness's accumulated chosen/rejected pairs (five instrumented runs, ~100 labeled decisions), targeting the action-repetition residue that survives corrective re-prompting, alongside the rationale-groundedness lint (the repeat guard itself is implemented and evaluated in §3); (3) scaling the benchmark toward live AndroidWorld-style episodic evaluation; (4) a supervised study with blind Arabic-speaking participants under institutional ethics review; (5) exploiting Qwen3.5's native vision channel as a screenshot fallback for WebViews and unlabeled controls — a capability our text-only pipeline deliberately excluded; (6) the reason-length budget sweep and prompt-V2.1 ablation.

## 5. Conclusion

For real-time assistive agents on constrained hardware, the operative levers are not model-generation upgrades but *decoding-level scaffolding*: a grammar-enforced, length-capped rationale generated ahead of the action restores most of chain-of-thought's decision quality at roughly 1/190th of its measured latency (1.71 s vs 328 s per decision), worked demonstrations move rare-but-critical safety behaviors from zero occurrence to reliable use, and a deterministic confirmation gate converts residual model error from a hazard into a spoken question. On our Arabic benchmark, this combination — Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with bounded reasoning and the lexical gate — reaches 80.0% step accuracy with 14.3% model-unsafe at 1.7 s per decision under prompt V2, and a targeted revision (V2.1) eliminates model-level unsafe actions entirely (0/14, measured executed-unsafe 0/14) at unchanged accuracy, and a one-retry executor repeat guard then reaches 90.0% accuracy at ~3.3 s per decision with both residual errors benign; the single repetition that survives an explicit corrective notice — acknowledged in the model's own rationale yet re-issued in its action — is the residue we assign to model weights, the natural target of the fine-tuning stage that follows. The failures we document along the way — action collapse, off-distribution thinking ablation, and the truncation masquerade — are, we believe, general hazards for anyone building or evaluating small-model agents, and the remedies are inexpensive.
